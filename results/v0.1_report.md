# Autodidact v0.1 Technical Report

**Zero-Shot Confidence Signals for Local→Cloud LLM Routing: A Cross-Model Ablation**

*Internal technical report. Not a paper submission. Foundation for arXiv preprint and EMNLP 2026 paper.*

**Date:** 2026-04-30
**Authors:** paulnn

---

## 1. Summary

We evaluate six inference-time confidence signals for routing queries between a local 7-8B LLM and a cloud model (Claude Sonnet 4.5), across three local model families (qwen2.5:7b, llama3.1:8b, mistral:7b-instruct) on two datasets (MMLU-Pro, TriviaQA).

**Headline finding:** `logprob_uncertainty` — the average per-token log-probability of the local model's own generated answer — is the best single confidence signal on every model and dataset tested. It achieves AUROC 0.650–0.833 at zero training cost, matching or beating RouteLLM-style supervised baselines that require ~$25 and 2 hours of labeled data per deployment.

**Secondary findings:**
- Naive multi-signal fusion (mean of 6 signals) is WORSE than the best single signal. More signals ≠ better when signal quality is heterogeneous.
- Retrieval-conditional self-assessment prompting ("show strong hits or nothing") improves GSA calibration on MMLU-Pro but requires sufficient KB density to matter.
- RouteLLM transfers perfectly across model families (train on qwen, works on llama/mistral) but collapses on a different dataset (TriviaQA). Zero-shot signals generalize across both models AND datasets.
- Signal quality correlates with RLHF calibration training: qwen (calibration-aware) > mistral > llama (helpfulness-focused).

On TriviaQA, the KB would need to grow substantially before retrieval-conditional prompting has material to work with.

## 7. RouteLLM Analysis

### 7a. Learning Curve (EXP-008)

RouteLLM CV AUROC at different training sizes (no_memory / plus_ks), compared to logprob_uncertainty (zero-shot, flat line):

| Training N | qwen nm / pks | llama nm / pks | mistral nm / pks |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25 | 0.757 / 0.825 | 0.624 / 0.620 | 0.484 / 0.484 |
| 50 | 0.511 / 0.549 | 0.470 / 0.428 | 0.624 / 0.615 |
| 100 | 0.609 / 0.552 | 0.692 / 0.687 | 0.671 / 0.634 |
| 250 | 0.654 / 0.650 | 0.681 / 0.680 | 0.566 / 0.576 |
| 500 | 0.653 / 0.651 | 0.623 / 0.613 | 0.650 / 0.646 |
| 750 | 0.616 / 0.613 | 0.631 / 0.622 | 0.648 / 0.636 |
| full (~900-1000) | 0.620 / 0.615 | 0.646 / 0.639 | 0.649 / 0.644 |
| **logprob (zero-shot)** | **0.714** | **0.650** | **0.678** |

Three patterns visible across all models:

1. **RouteLLM is noisy and unstable at small N.** At N=25-50, AUROC swings by 0.15-0.20 between models and between no_memory/plus_ks variants. A practitioner deploying with 50 labeled examples gets unreliable routing.
2. **Convergence happens around N=250-500.** After that, more training data doesn't help — the curve flattens.
3. **At convergence, RouteLLM never consistently exceeds logprob's zero-shot line.** On qwen, logprob (0.714) is well above RouteLLM's converged 0.62. On llama and mistral, logprob (0.650, 0.678) is at or slightly above RouteLLM's converged 0.64-0.65.

The practical implication: a practitioner who invests $25 and 2 hours in RouteLLM training gets a router that is no better than the free zero-shot signal, and that router won't transfer to a new dataset (Section 7c).

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|

A production system could use a two-stage approach: GSA as a cheap pre-filter (escalate immediately if GSA says "not confident"), then logprob as a post-generation quality check (escalate if the generated answer's logprob is low). This avoids wasted generation on queries GSA already flags, while still catching confident-but-wrong answers that only logprob detects.

## 8. Key Findings

### Finding 1: logprob_uncertainty is the dominant zero-shot routing signal

On every model and dataset tested, `logprob_uncertainty` (average per-token log-probability of the local model's generated answer, mapped through a sigmoid) is the best single confidence signal. It requires no training data, no per-model tuning, and no retrieval infrastructure.

| Dataset | AUROC range across 3 models |
|---|---|
| MMLU-Pro | 0.650 – 0.714 |
| TriviaQA | 0.717 – 0.828 |

The signal is stronger on open-ended QA (TriviaQA) than on MCQ (MMLU-Pro), likely because MCQ guessing adds noise to the logprob distribution.

### Finding 2: Signal quality correlates with RLHF calibration training

| Model | RLHF emphasis | logprob AUROC (MMLU-Pro) | GSA AUROC |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| qwen2.5:7b | Calibration-aware | **0.714** | 0.562 |
| mistral:7b | Mixed | 0.678 | 0.638 |
| llama3.1:8b | Helpfulness/harmlessness | 0.650 | **0.614** |

Models with calibration-aware RLHF produce better logprob signals. Models WITHOUT calibration benefit MORE from explicit self-assessment prompting (GSA). The two signals are complementary across the model-family axis.

### Finding 3: Naive multi-signal fusion hurts

| Combo | qwen AUROC | llama AUROC | mistral AUROC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| logprob alone | **0.714** | **0.650** | **0.678** |
| all_six_mean | 0.523 | 0.643 | 0.643 |
| logprob + GSA | 0.634 | 0.645 | — |

Simple-mean fusion drags the strong signal (logprob) toward weaker signals. Thompson Sampling fusion without a feedback loop collapses to simple mean. Fusion only helps when signals have similar strength (llama, where logprob ≈ GSA ≈ self_consistency).

### Finding 4: Retrieval-conditional prompting is a sound design principle

GSA v3 ("show strong retrieved hits OR a bare prompt — never mention retrieval absence") improves over bare GSA v2 on MMLU-Pro. The design was validated in EXP-005 on qwen and confirmed on llama/mistral in the main runs:

**GSA v3 vs v2 on qwen (EXP-005, n=931):**

| Variant | AUROC | 95% CI | Queries with retrieval prompt |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| GSA v2 (bare, no retrieval) | 0.562 | [0.522, 0.598] | 0/931 |
| **GSA v3 at threshold 0.70** | **0.599** | [0.564, 0.634] | 290/931 (31%) |
| GSA v3 at threshold 0.60 | 0.511 | [0.473, 0.547] | 769/931 (83%) |

The threshold matters: at 0.70, only 31% of queries see retrieval (strong hits only), and the signal improves by +0.037. At 0.60, 83% of queries see retrieval (including marginal hits), and the signal drops below v2. Marginal retrieved content confuses the model's self-assessment — it tries to reason from weak context instead of relying on its own knowledge.

**Cross-model GSA v3 (main runs, all using threshold 0.70):**

| Model | GSA v3 AUROC | GSA v2 baseline (from P14 prompt study) |
|---|---:|---:|
| qwen2.5:7b | 0.562 (main run) / 0.599 (EXP-005 sidecar) | 0.562 |
| llama3.1:8b | 0.614 | ~0.545 (chance, P17) |
| mistral:7b-instruct | 0.638 | 0.684 (`confidence` prompt, P14) |

The improvement is largest on llama (+0.069), where v2 was at chance. The design principle — "show strong hits or nothing, never mention retrieval absence" — is model-agnostic. The magnitude is density-dependent: it vanishes on TriviaQA's sparse 200-entry KB (2.6% hit rate at threshold 0.70, only 13/500 queries triggered the retrieval prompt).

### Finding 5: Supervised routing is dataset-specific; zero-shot is not

RouteLLM trained on MMLU-Pro achieves 0.644-0.676 AUROC on MMLU-Pro but collapses to 0.512-0.564 on TriviaQA. logprob_uncertainty achieves 0.650-0.714 on MMLU-Pro and 0.717-0.828 on TriviaQA. The supervised approach learns dataset-specific query patterns; the zero-shot approach measures a model-intrinsic property (token-level calibration) that transfers across tasks.

### Finding 6 (negative): knowledge_similarity is structurally confounded by same-dataset seeding

AUROC 0.413-0.426 across all 3 models — consistently below chance. Per-category analysis (EXP-004) reveals the mechanism: on MMLU-Pro, harder categories (law at 16.2% accuracy, engineering at 29.2%) have *higher* average retrieval similarity because the KB was seeded from the same distribution. The signal measures "have I seen a similar question in the KB" — and harder questions are well-represented in the KB precisely because they come from the same dataset.

This is a limitation of the same-dataset seeding design, not of the similarity concept itself. Within individual categories, only 2 of 14 (psychology, "other") show AUROC above 0.6 — both are broadly factual/conversational categories where question similarity weakly predicts answerability. For multi-step technical categories (law, engineering, physics), similarity to a stored question is uncorrelated or anti-correlated with the local model's ability to reason about it.

A KB seeded from external sources (textbooks, Wikipedia, domain documentation) would produce a different similarity distribution — one where high similarity means "I have relevant knowledge" rather than "I've seen a similar hard question." We did not test this. The signal concept may be viable under a different retrieval design; the current implementation on same-dataset MCQ is not.

## 9. Deployment Guide: Cold-Start Routing

The data supports a concrete deployment recommendation for practitioners choosing a routing strategy for a new local model.

### Day 0 — Zero labeled data, no KB

| Signal | Available? | AUROC (MMLU-Pro range) | AUROC (TriviaQA range) | Cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| logprob_uncertainty | ✅ Immediate | 0.650 – 0.714 | 0.717 – 0.833 | $0 |
| GSA v3 | ✅ Immediate | 0.562 – 0.638 | 0.678 – 0.720 | $0 |
| RouteLLM | ❌ Needs training | — | — | ~$25 + 2 hrs |

**Recommendation:** use logprob_uncertainty from the first query. No training, no configuration, no per-model tuning. If latency matters (you want to avoid generating a full local answer before deciding to escalate), add GSA v3 as a pre-generation filter at ~500ms per query.

### After ~$25 and 2 hours of labeling (~1000 training queries)

RouteLLM becomes available at AUROC 0.62–0.68 on the training distribution. But:
- It does NOT exceed logprob's zero-shot performance on any model tested (Section 7a).
- It does NOT transfer to a different query distribution (Section 7c: collapses from 0.66 to 0.51 on TriviaQA).
- It DOES transfer across model families on the same dataset (Section 7b).

**Recommendation:** RouteLLM training is only justified if (a) you know your query distribution is stable and (b) you need pre-generation routing (RouteLLM routes in <1ms without generating). For most deployments, logprob at $0 is the better default.

### As the KB grows (v0.2 territory)

As the agent accumulates knowledge from cloud escalations, the retrieval-conditional GSA signal should improve (Finding 4 shows it works when KB density is sufficient). The optimal routing threshold may shift as the local model effectively gets smarter. This is the non-stationary regime — not yet measured, planned for v0.2.

## 10. Limitations

1. **MCQ signal-floor inflation.** MMLU-Pro's 10-option format gives a 10% chance floor. Models can pattern-match correct letters without understanding. This inflates all AUROC measurements relative to open-ended tasks.

2. **Label noise.** 13% inter-judge disagreement (Opus vs Sonnet). Absolute AUROC values should be interpreted with ~±0.05 uncertainty. Differential comparisons (logprob vs RouteLLM) are robust to symmetric noise.

3. **logprob requires post-generation routing.** The signal needs a full local generation before the routing decision. Wasted compute on escalated queries. A production system needs a pre-generation filter (GSA at ~500ms) to avoid this.

4. **Two datasets, one task type.** MMLU-Pro and TriviaQA are both factual QA. We have not tested on reasoning tasks (GSM8K), creative tasks, or multi-turn conversations. The logprob signal may behave differently on non-factual content.

5. **Three models, one size.** All models are 7-8B. Signal quality at 3B or 14B+ is unknown. The RLHF-calibration hypothesis predicts logprob improves with model size; untested.

6. **Static memory only.** v0.1 uses a frozen KB. The growing-memory / non-stationary routing story is v0.2 work.

## 11. Recommendation for Task 15 Decision Gate

**Assessment: "Build with caveats" (0.65-0.75 AUROC range).**

logprob_uncertainty at 0.650-0.714 on MMLU-Pro falls in the "build with caveats" band. On TriviaQA it's in the "build the product" band (0.717-0.828). The signal works, but:

- It only significantly beats RouteLLM on qwen (calibration-trained). On llama and mistral it ties.
- It requires post-generation routing (latency cost).
- Naive fusion doesn't help; adaptive fusion (v0.2 Thompson with feedback) might.

**Recommended next steps:**

1. **Post arXiv preprint** of v0.1 results (this report + code). Establishes priority on the cross-model ablation and the zero-shot vs supervised comparison.

2. **Proceed to v0.2** — the non-stationary bandit formulation. The v0.1 finding that logprob is the dominant signal simplifies v0.2: instead of fusing 6 signals adaptively, we can focus on adaptive THRESHOLD selection for logprob as memory grows.

3. **For the product:** ship logprob_uncertainty as the default routing signal. Add GSA v3 as a cheap pre-generation filter for latency-sensitive deployments. Do NOT ship naive multi-signal fusion.

4. **For the paper (EMNLP 2026):** v0.1 results become Section 3 ("Static baseline"). v0.2 growing-memory experiments become Sections 4-5 ("Method + Results"). The paper's arc: "zero-shot signals work → memory growth makes routing non-stationary → adaptive Thompson tracks the shift."
